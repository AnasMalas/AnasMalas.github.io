Download and flashing steps

Easiest: the easy flasher for Windows, macOS and Linux downloads everything and flashes the card for you. Or by hand:

  1. Download the new firmware (a .bin file).
  2. Get a flashing tool. Windows: WCHISPTool from WCH. macOS and Linux: wchisp.
  3. Unplug the card. Flashing needs both USB ports: DATA carries data only and does not power the card. Plug DATA into this computer first.
  4. Hold BOOT and plug a charger into POWER, then let go. If the display lights up, the card started normally instead: unplug POWER and try again, pressing BOOT harder.
  5. WCHISPTool: choose CH32X035, pick the file, and press Download. wchisp: run wchisp flash with the file.
  6. Unplug POWER, plug it back in, and connect here again. This banner then says it is the latest version.

Front-panel warning letters

WARN spells one Morse letter, then pauses. A dash is three times as long as a dot. If several warnings are active, each letter plays in turn. R plays three times; T plays three times, or until LOAD is pressed if the dip cut the load.

R  ·–·
Request failed: the charger refused it and the previous contract remains, or it agreed three times without delivering the voltage and the card stays at 5 V.
S  ···
Save unavailable: the flash page for the next settings save could not be prepared or written, so changes may not be remembered.
T  –
Tripped: the supply dropped out and it showed. It cut the load (T stays until LOAD is pressed again), restarted the display, or ended the PD session. A brief dip the board rode through is not shown.
F  ··–·
Fallback: earlier boots lost power while restoring the remembered setting, so this boot runs a fixed PDO, 5 V, or no USB-PD at all. Press APPLY to retry the remembered setting, or adjust with the +/− keys first. Unplugging and reconnecting also retries it in full.

During display power limiting, THERM LIM stays on, TEMP WARN blinks, and the digits drop their leading zeros. These indicate the regulator power model, not a measured board temperature. PPS CV/CL follows the source’s reported PPS status; both stay off if that status is unavailable.

USB-PD primer — PDOs, contracts, PPS, AVS, EPR and resets

Negotiation

CC1/CC2 first establish attachment and cable orientation; VBUS begins at default 5 V. The source sends Source_Capabilities, a list of PDOs. The sink requests one advertised operating point. Accept approves the request and PS_RDY says the requested rail is ready. A link-level GoodCRC only confirms that a PD packet was received—it does not approve its contents.

Capability types

Fixed PDO offers one voltage with a current ceiling. PPS is an adjustable SPR range; the sink requests voltage and current inside it. AVS is the EPR adjustable range and is commonly power-limited, so available current can fall as voltage rises. EPR is the protocol mode that exposes extended-power capabilities such as 28/36/48 V and EPR AVS.

How to read this page

Requested is what the card asks for. Contract is what the source accepted. Measured V/A comes from the card ADC and may differ because of tolerance, droop, or calibration. Above a fixed contract's current limit, source over-current protection normally removes or cycles VBUS. Under PPS, the source can instead enter CL mode and reduce voltage to hold the current limit. A Hard Reset restarts PD and can also cycle VBUS; LOAD permission is cleared for safety.
